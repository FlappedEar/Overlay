Track segments and the Corner Analyzer

Segments divide a lap into corners and straights. Approved segments are the basis for sector times, the theoretical best, time losses, the Corner Analyzer and the day report.

Automatic segments

When a layout in the selected comparison group has no approved segments yet, the application proposes segments from the day's best lap and approves them automatically on that lap's run. The status bar reports N segment(s) created automatically from the best lap. This happens once per layout and best lap; segments you revoke afterwards are not recreated.

Automatic segments are a starting point. Review them on a clean lap before relying on corner results.

Reviewing segments

Open a complete, eligible lap and click Review segments. The TRACK SEGMENTS panel has two parts.

Approved segments

The box at the top shows the approved revision and its segments: name, type (corner, straight or sector) and start–end distance in metres. Each approved segment can be changed:

  • Edit: name, type, Start (m) and End (m). Pick on map lets you click the boundary on the track line instead of typing it. Move adjoining segments with shared boundaries (on by default) keeps neighbours connected.
  • Split: split at a distance or a point picked on the map.
  • Merge next: join with the following segment.
  • Revoke: remove the approval.

Undo and Redo work for segment edits during the session. Any change creates a new revision; results calculated with an earlier revision become out of date and are recalculated.

If a map pick lands where another part of the track passes close by, enter the distance instead.

Proposals

Below the approved box, the lap's automatic proposals are listed. Each shows a state (Proposed, Approved, Rejected, Overlaps approved), the type and turn angle (for example Corner · 85° left), the boundaries with their uncertainty, and the geometric apex. Notes flag uncertain boundaries, for example Corners connect without a straight or Near a GPS gap in this lap.

  • Approve, Reject/Restore and Edit act on one proposal; Approve all approves every proposal.
  • Recompute proposals calculates them again.
  • Proposals and rejections are not saved; only approved segments are.

On the map, approved segments are green, corner proposals blue, straights grey and uncertainty windows amber.

Segments are stored per run and tied to its track configuration. Segments approved for a different configuration are listed separately and not applied; Discard them removes them.

When review is not possible

  • The lap is not a complete timed lap.
  • The lap has incomplete GPS or leaves the run's usual line; open a clean lap.
  • The run's layout, direction or timing gate is not identified; set them under Correct grouping.
  • No proposal could be made: the lap turns continuously, no corner was found, or there would be more than 64 segments.

Corner Analyzer

In lap comparison, click Corner Analyzer. It needs the same approved segment revision on both laps; otherwise it says No matching approved segments for these two laps. When opened from the theoretical best, time losses or the day report, it uses the segments of the run those results were calculated on, and notes that boundaries can shift by a few metres on other laps.

Click a segment to zoom the charts and map to it. Lap A here… and Lap B here… open that lap at the segment start, with video when available.

For the selected segment, the table compares A, B and Δ (A−B):

MetricMeaning
Sector timeTime through the segment
Entry, Apex, Minimum, Exit speed (corners)Speed at entry, at the geometric apex, the lowest speed (which can differ from the apex), and at exit
Entry, Top, Lowest, Exit speed (straights)Speed at entry, maximum, minimum, and at exit
Braking pointPosition (m) where braking starts. Positive Δ: A brakes later
Throttle pickupPosition (m) where throttle is applied again after the lift
Heart rateMean heart rate over the segment
Trail brakingTime braking while cornering, with a strip showing braking (red), cornering (blue) and both (violet)

Braking and throttle are taken from the recorded pedal channels. Without them they are inferred from longitudinal G and marked as inferred; measured and inferred values are never compared with each other. More overlap of braking and cornering is not automatically better or safer.
