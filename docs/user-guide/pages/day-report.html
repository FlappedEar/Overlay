Day results and reports

Day-level views summarise every eligible lap in the selected comparison group: what your best lap could gain, where time was lost, how sessions progressed, and what the car and driver recorded.

Results that depend on corners and sectors need approved segments. Statistics need at least three laps. When a result cannot be calculated, the view says why; a missing result is never shown as zero.

Theoretical best

Theoretical best… opens Where your best lap can improve.

  • The summary shows YOUR BEST LAP, THEORETICAL BEST, LAPS TODAY (typical lap time and spread) and AVAILABLE (the difference).
  • The theoretical best adds up the fastest recorded time in each approved sector, taken from any eligible lap of the group. It combines fragments of different laps and does not prove that the whole lap can be driven that fast.
  • The map colours each segment by how much time your best lap loses there. Click a segment for its details; double-click to compare.
  • WHERE THE TIME IS lists segments by the time available, largest first, with the lap that was fastest there. Click a row to open that lap (A) against your best lap (B) in the Corner Analyzer.
  • For a selected segment, a lap-to-lap variability box shows the spread of braking point, apex, minimum and exit speeds, throttle pickup and line. A line spread within GPS accuracy is marked as not distinguishable from GPS error.

No total is shown when any sector has no fully covered time on any eligible lap.

Largest time losses

Time losses… lists where laps lost the most time against the group's best lap, per approved segment.

  • By default each run's best lap is compared. Tick Include every eligible lap to compare all of them.
  • Columns: rank, Loss, Segment, Lap and Coverage (how much of the window both laps' data covered). Windows without full coverage are left out and counted.
  • The straight after a corner is a separate window, so time lost on the exit is not counted in the corner.
  • Click a loss to open that lap against the reference in the Corner Analyzer, zoomed to the segment. Closing the comparison returns to the list.

The 50 largest losses are listed. An observed loss is not a guaranteed or necessarily safe gain.

Run progression

Progression… opens Run progression with three tabs.

Laps

One card per run in recording order: first recorded time, best lap (click to open it), the change of best lap against the previous run (negative is faster), a box plot of all eligible lap times on a shared scale (whiskers: minimum and maximum; box: middle 50 %; line: median), and the run's notes, conditions, setup changes and excluded laps. No outliers are removed.

By section

A grid with approved sections as rows and sessions as columns. Each cell shows the median time, spread and lap count. The quickest typical session for a section is green; slower sessions are shown in increasingly bright orange. Cells with fewer than three laps show only the count. Click a cell to list its laps and open one.

Car & driver

Recorded temperatures and heart rate per session, on a shared scale per channel, in recording order. Recording gaps stay open.

  • Heart rate: per run mean, minimum–maximum, samples and coverage; per-lap chips open the lap with the heart-rate channel added.
  • Temperatures (oil, coolant, intake air, gearbox and other channels whose name contains temp): a trend chart, a table of mean, minimum–maximum and coverage per session, and continuously recorded cooling of at least 5° over at least 30 seconds, marked in blue.
  • WITH LAP PERFORMANCE: how each temperature relates to lap time and to strong acceleration across eligible laps (Spearman rank correlation), with a scatter plot. It needs at least 8 laps with at least 80 % sensor coverage, and warns when temperature also follows the time of day. It does not establish a critical temperature or a cause.

Implausible values (heart rate outside 30–230 bpm, temperatures outside −40 to 250 °C, exact zeros) are excluded and counted.

Where to look next

Up to three areas to inspect, at most one per segment. Each states what was observed, a hypothesis kept separate from the observation, and which two laps to compare where. The kinds are:

  • Best lap against the fastest sector: your best lap is at least 0.05 s slower than the fastest recorded time in a sector.
  • Repeated loss: at least three session-best laps each lose at least 0.05 s in the same segment.
  • Braking-point spread: the measured braking point varies by at least 10 m across at least three laps. This does not say whether earlier or later braking is faster or safe.
  • Lowest-speed spread: the minimum speed varies by at least 5 % of its median.

If nothing qualifies, the card says No loss, sector gap or spread is large enough to single out.

How typical values and spread are calculated

"Typical" is the median. "Spread" is the interquartile range: the width of the middle half of the values (25th to 75th percentile). At least three values are needed. There is no percentage score.
